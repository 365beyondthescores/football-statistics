# What Is Expected Goals (xG)?

## Definition

Expected Goals (xG) is a statistical metric used to estimate the probability that a shot will result in a goal.

The metric is usually expressed as a value between 0 and 1 for an individual shot.

For example, an xG value of 0.20 means that a statistical model estimates the shot has approximately a 20% probability of resulting in a goal.

## How Is xG Calculated?

Different data providers may use different models and variables.

An xG model can consider factors such as:

- Shot location
- Distance from goal
- Shot angle
- Type of assist
- Body part used
- Defensive pressure
- Match situation
- Whether the shot follows a set piece or open play

The exact methodology can vary between providers.

## What Does xG Tell Us?

xG can help provide additional context beyond the final score.

For example, two teams may finish a match 1–1 while creating very different qualities of scoring opportunities.

One team may have created several high-quality chances, while the other may have scored from a lower-probability opportunity.

## xG Example

Imagine a team creates five shots:

- Shot 1: 0.05 xG
- Shot 2: 0.10 xG
- Shot 3: 0.30 xG
- Shot 4: 0.20 xG
- Shot 5: 0.35 xG

The combined xG is:

**1.00 xG**

This represents the total expected goal value of those chances according to the model.

It does not mean the team is guaranteed to score exactly one goal.

## xG Is Not a Prediction

xG should not be interpreted as a guarantee of the final result.

A team with 2.5 xG can score zero goals, while another team with 0.5 xG can score two goals.

Football contains randomness, individual skill, goalkeeping, defensive actions, and many other factors that statistical models cannot perfectly capture.

## Why xG Is Useful

xG can help analysts examine:

- Chance quality
- Attacking performance
- Finishing performance
- Team performance
- Match trends
- Differences between results and underlying chances

## Important Considerations

xG values should be interpreted carefully.

Different providers may produce different xG values because their models, data sources, and methodologies can differ.

When comparing xG statistics, always consider the source and methodology.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does xG stand for?

xG stands for **Expected Goals**.

### Is xG the number of goals a team will score?

No. xG is a statistical estimate of the quality and probability of scoring chances.

### Can a team win despite having lower xG?

Yes. A team can score from fewer or lower-probability chances and still win the match.

### Is xG the same across every statistics provider?

Not necessarily. Different providers can use different models and data.
