# What Is a Key Pass in Football?

## Definition

A key pass is a pass that directly leads to a teammate taking a shot.

Key passes are commonly used to evaluate a player's chance-creation contribution.

The exact definition can vary between football data providers.

## Key Pass vs Assist

A key pass and an assist are related but different statistics.

An **assist** is generally awarded when a player's action directly contributes to a goal.

A **key pass** can lead to a shot without resulting in a goal.

For example, if a player makes a pass that allows a teammate to shoot but the shot is saved or missed, the pass may still be recorded as a key pass.

## Example

Imagine a player creates four shooting opportunities:

- Chance 1 → shot saved
- Chance 2 → shot goes wide
- Chance 3 → goal scored
- Chance 4 → shot blocked

Depending on the provider's definition, these passes may be recorded as key passes.

The pass leading to the goal may also receive an assist.

## Why Are Key Passes Important?

Key passes can help measure a player's ability to create shooting opportunities.

They can provide context about:

- Creativity
- Chance creation
- Playmaking
- Attacking involvement
- Passing in dangerous areas

However, key passes do not measure the quality of every chance equally.

## Key Passes and Expected Assists (xA)

Expected Assists (xA) provides another way to evaluate chance creation.

A key pass generally records whether a pass led to a shot.

xA attempts to estimate the likelihood that the resulting chance will become a goal.

For example, two key passes may create very different opportunities:

- Key Pass A → low-quality shot from distance
- Key Pass B → high-quality chance close to goal

Both may count as key passes, but their expected goal value can be very different.

## Key Passes and Shots

Key passes are closely related to shot creation because they directly precede a shot.

A player's key-pass total can help analysts understand how frequently that player creates shooting opportunities for teammates.

## Example of Player Creativity

Consider two players over the same number of matches:

### Player A

- 8 key passes
- 2 assists

### Player B

- 25 key passes
- 1 assist

Player B created more shooting opportunities according to the key-pass statistic, but Player A recorded more assists.

This demonstrates that chance creation and final outcomes are not always the same.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a key pass?

A key pass is generally a pass that directly leads to a teammate taking a shot.

### Is a key pass an assist?

Not necessarily.

A key pass can lead to a shot that does not result in a goal.

### Can a key pass become an assist?

Yes.

If the resulting shot leads to a goal and meets the relevant assist criteria, the pass may also be recorded as an assist.

### Do key passes measure chance quality?

Not by themselves.

Key passes measure shot-creating actions, while metrics such as xA can provide additional information about the quality of the chances created.

### Is the definition of a key pass the same everywhere?

No.

Different statistical providers may use different definitions and data-collection methodologies.

## How to Interpret Key Passes

Key passes are most useful when combined with other attacking metrics:

**Key Passes → xA → Shots → xG → Goals**

This provides a broader view of a player's creative and attacking contribution.

## Data Methodology

Football statistics can vary between providers because of differences in data sources, event definitions, and collection methods.

When comparing key-pass statistics, always consider the source and methodology.

## Summary

Key passes are a useful statistic for understanding how frequently a player creates shooting opportunities for teammates.

They complement assists and expected-assist metrics by focusing on the creation of shots rather than only the final scoring outcome.
