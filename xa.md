# What Is Expected Assists (xA)?

## Definition

Expected Assists (xA) is a statistical metric used to estimate the likelihood that a pass or chance-creating action will result in a goal for the receiving player.

Unlike traditional assists, which only record whether a goal was actually scored, xA focuses on the quality of the opportunities created by a player.

## How Does xA Work?

An xA model generally evaluates the quality of a chance created by a pass.

Depending on the data provider and methodology, factors may include:

- Location of the resulting shot
- Type of pass
- Angle of the pass
- Distance of the pass
- Type of attacking action
- Position of defenders
- Match situation

Different providers can use different models and definitions.

## xA vs Assists

There is an important difference between assists and expected assists.

**Assist:**
Records an actual goal contribution.

**xA:**
Estimates the quality of the scoring opportunity created by a player's pass.

For example, a player may create several high-quality chances but finish a match with zero assists because teammates fail to score.

That player could still record a relatively high xA value.

## Example

Imagine a player creates three shooting opportunities:

- Chance 1: 0.10 xA
- Chance 2: 0.25 xA
- Chance 3: 0.40 xA

The combined xA is:

**0.75 xA**

This represents the estimated goal value of the chances created according to the statistical model.

It does not mean the player's team is guaranteed to score 0.75 goals.

## Why Is xA Useful?

xA can help analysts evaluate a player's creative contribution.

It can provide additional context when examining:

- Chance creation
- Passing quality
- Creative play
- Attacking contribution
- Playmaking
- Team performance

## xA and Player Performance

Traditional assists can sometimes be influenced by the finishing ability of teammates.

A player may make an excellent pass but receive no assist if the resulting shot is missed.

xA can help provide additional context by measuring the quality of the chance created rather than only the final outcome.

## Important Limitations

xA should not be treated as a perfect measurement of creativity.

Different statistical providers may use different models, data sources, and definitions.

A player's creative contribution can also involve actions that are difficult to capture in a single metric.

For this reason, xA is most useful when considered alongside other statistics and match context.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does xA stand for?

xA stands for **Expected Assists**.

### Is xA the same as assists?

No.

An assist records an actual goal contribution, while xA estimates the quality of scoring opportunities created by a player's passes.

### Can a player have high xA but few assists?

Yes.

A player can create several high-quality opportunities while teammates fail to convert them into goals.

### Is xA the same across all data providers?

Not necessarily.

Different providers can use different models and methodologies to calculate xA.

### Does high xA guarantee more assists?

No.

xA is a statistical estimate and does not guarantee that a chance will become a goal or an assist.

## Data Sources and Methodology

Expected Assists (xA) is not calculated using one universal methodology across all football statistics providers.

Different providers may use different event data, models, definitions, and assumptions when estimating the expected value of a pass that leads to a shot.

An xA model may consider factors such as:

- Location of the resulting shot
- Angle of the shot
- Type of attacking action
- Location from which the pass was played
- Type of pass
- Defensive pressure
- Match situation
- Other contextual information

The exact variables and model structure can differ between providers.

### How to Compare xA Data

When comparing xA statistics, consider:

1. The data provider
2. The definition of an assist
3. The model methodology
4. The type of event data used
5. Whether the statistic is reported per match, per 90 minutes, or as a total

Different models can assign different xA values to the same chance.

### Important Note

xA is a model-based estimate and should not be interpreted as a guaranteed number of assists.

The explanations in this knowledge hub describe the general concept of Expected Assists and are not intended to reproduce the proprietary methodology of any specific provider.

For published statistics, refer to the original data provider and its methodology.
