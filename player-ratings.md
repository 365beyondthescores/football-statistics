# What Are Player Ratings in Football?

## Definition

A football player rating is a numerical score used to summarize a player's performance during a match or over a period of matches.

Player ratings are commonly presented as numbers, such as 6.5, 7.2, or 8.4.

The exact meaning of a rating depends on the data provider, scoring system, and methodology used.

## How Are Player Ratings Calculated?

Different football data providers use different methods to calculate player ratings.

A rating system may consider statistics such as:

- Goals
- Assists
- Shots
- Expected Goals (xG)
- Expected Assists (xA)
- Key passes
- Passing
- Defensive actions
- Tackles
- Interceptions
- Ball recoveries
- Duels
- Playing time
- Match result

Some systems also use advanced statistical models to evaluate player performance.

## Why Can Player Ratings Differ?

Two websites may give the same player different ratings for the same match.

This can happen because they may use:

- Different data sources
- Different statistical models
- Different weighting systems
- Different definitions
- Different approaches to evaluating player contributions

Therefore, player ratings should always be interpreted according to their methodology.

## Example

Imagine a player receives these ratings:

- Provider A: 7.8
- Provider B: 7.2
- Provider C: 8.1

The different values do not necessarily mean that one provider is incorrect.

Each provider may use a different model to evaluate performance.

## Player Ratings and Goals

Goals can have a major influence on player ratings.

However, scoring a goal does not automatically mean that a player had the best overall performance.

A midfielder may control the game through passing and defensive work without scoring.

A defender may make important interceptions and recoveries without contributing a goal or assist.

This is why player ratings should be considered alongside the player's position and tactical role.

## Player Ratings and xG

Expected Goals (xG) can provide additional context when evaluating attacking players.

For example, a striker may score two goals from relatively low-probability chances.

Another striker may score once while creating several high-quality opportunities.

Player ratings can incorporate these performances differently depending on the provider's model.

## Player Ratings and xA

Expected Assists (xA) can help evaluate creative contributions.

A player may create several high-quality opportunities for teammates without receiving an actual assist.

This can provide useful context when interpreting a player's overall rating.

## Player Ratings and Passing

Passing statistics can provide additional context for midfielders and defenders.

Relevant metrics may include:

- Passing accuracy
- Progressive passes
- Key passes
- Pass volume

A player with lower passing accuracy may still have a strong performance if they attempt difficult progressive or creative passes.

See also:

- [Passing Accuracy](passing-accuracy.md)

## Player Ratings and Defensive Performance

Defensive contributions can also influence player ratings.

Depending on the provider, relevant statistics may include:

- Tackles
- Interceptions
- Clearances
- Blocks
- Recoveries
- Defensive duels
- Goals prevented

A defender's performance should therefore not be judged only by goals and assists.

## Player Ratings and Clean Sheets

Clean sheets may provide context for defenders and goalkeepers.

However, a clean sheet is a team result and does not automatically mean that every defender or goalkeeper performed equally well.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a player rating in football?

A player rating is a numerical score used to summarize an individual player's performance.

### What is considered a good player rating?

There is no universal standard.

A rating considered strong by one provider may use a different scale or methodology from another provider.

### Why do player ratings differ between websites?

Different providers can use different data sources, statistical models, weighting systems, and definitions.

### Does scoring a goal guarantee a high rating?

No.

Goals can strongly influence ratings, but overall performance can involve many other factors.

### Are player ratings objective?

They are based on statistical data and/or analytical models, but the methodology and weighting decisions can vary between providers.

### Should player ratings be used alone?

No.

Ratings are most useful when considered alongside the underlying statistics, tactical role, match context, and methodology.

## How to Interpret Player Ratings

A useful analysis can combine:

**Player Rating → Key Statistics → Role → Tactical Context → Match Performance**

This provides more context than looking at the final rating alone.

## Data Methodology

Player ratings are not standardized across the football statistics industry.

Different providers may use different definitions, datasets, algorithms, and weighting systems.

When comparing ratings, always identify the provider and understand how the rating is calculated.

## Summary

Player ratings provide a convenient way to summarize football performance numerically.

However, a rating is only meaningful when its methodology and context are understood.

For deeper analysis, player ratings should be considered alongside statistics such as xG, xA, passing, progression, chance creation, defensive actions, and match context.
