# What Are Shots in Football?

## Definition

A shot is an attempt by a player to score a goal.

Shots are one of the most commonly used statistics when evaluating attacking performance in football.

## What Does the Shots Statistic Measure?

The total shots statistic generally counts attempts made by players toward the opponent's goal.

Depending on the data provider, definitions and classifications may differ.

Shots can include attempts that:

- Go on target
- Go wide
- Hit the post or crossbar
- Are blocked
- Result in a goal

## Shots vs Shots on Target

Shots and shots on target are different statistics.

**Shots** represent the total number of recorded attempts.

**Shots on target** generally represent attempts that are directed toward the goal and would enter the goal if not stopped by the goalkeeper or another relevant defensive intervention.

A team can have many shots but relatively few shots on target.

## Example

Consider a match with these statistics:

- Team A: 15 shots
- Team B: 8 shots

Team A attempted more shots.

However, this does not automatically mean Team A created better scoring opportunities.

For example:

- Team A: 15 shots, 3 on target
- Team B: 8 shots, 5 on target

Team B had fewer total attempts but more shots on target.

## Shot Volume and Shot Quality

The number of shots alone does not tell the entire story.

A team may take many low-quality shots from outside the penalty area while another team creates fewer but higher-quality chances.

This is why shot volume is often analyzed together with **Expected Goals (xG)**.

## Shots and xG

Expected Goals (xG) estimates the probability that a shot will result in a goal.

For example, consider two teams:

- Team A: 20 shots, 0.8 xG
- Team B: 8 shots, 1.7 xG

Team A attempted more shots, but Team B created higher-quality opportunities according to the xG model.

This demonstrates why shot count should be interpreted together with shot quality.

## Shots and Possession

Possession can provide context for shot statistics.

A team with more possession may have more opportunities to attempt shots.

However, a team with less possession can still produce more dangerous attacks through transitions and counter-attacks.

## Why Are Shots Useful?

Shot statistics can help analysts understand:

- Attacking volume
- Offensive activity
- Chance creation
- Shooting frequency
- Team attacking patterns
- Player involvement in attacks

However, shots should be combined with other metrics for a more complete picture.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a shot in football?

A shot is an attempt by a player to score a goal.

### Are all shots shots on target?

No.

Shots can be wide, blocked, hit the post, or otherwise fail to meet the definition of a shot on target.

### Does having more shots mean a team played better?

Not necessarily.

The quality and location of the shots are also important.

### Why can a team have more shots but lose?

A team may take many low-quality attempts while its opponent creates fewer but better scoring opportunities.

### How is shots different from xG?

Shots measure the number of attempts, while xG estimates the quality or scoring probability of those attempts.

## How to Interpret Shot Statistics

A useful analysis can combine:

**Shots → Shots on Target → Shot Location → xG → Goals**

Looking at these statistics together provides more context than considering shot volume alone.

## Summary

Shots are an important indicator of attacking activity, but the number of attempts does not automatically reflect the quality of a team's performance.

For a deeper understanding, shot volume should be considered alongside shot accuracy, chance quality, xG, possession, tactics, and match context.

## Data Sources and Methodology

Shot statistics are based on recorded attempts toward the goal.

Different football data providers may use different event definitions and classification rules when recording shots.

A shot classification may take into account factors such as:

- The player's attempt
- Whether the attempt is directed toward goal
- Whether the attempt is blocked
- Whether the attempt results in a goal
- The location of the attempt
- The match event sequence

The exact classification of an attempt can vary between providers.

### How to Compare Shot Data

When comparing shot statistics, consider:

1. The data provider
2. The definition of a shot
3. How blocked attempts are classified
4. Whether own goals and deflections are treated differently
5. The competition and match context

### Important Note

Shot totals should be interpreted together with other metrics such as shots on target, xG, and match context.

A high number of shots does not necessarily indicate that a team created high-quality scoring opportunities.

For published statistics, refer to the original data provider and its methodology.
