# What Are Shots in Football?

## Definition

A shot is an attempt by a player to score a goal.

Shots are one of the most commonly used statistics when evaluating attacking activity in football.

The total shots statistic measures the number of recorded attempts made by a team or player during a match.

In simple terms:

**Shots measure attacking volume.**

However, the number of shots alone does not tell us how dangerous those attempts were.

## What Does the Shots Statistic Measure?

The total shots statistic generally counts recorded attempts made by players toward the opponent's goal.

Depending on the data provider, definitions and classifications may differ.

Shots can include attempts that:

- Go on target
- Go wide
- Hit the post or crossbar
- Are blocked
- Result in a goal

Because statistical providers can use different event definitions, shot totals may vary slightly between sources.

## Shots vs Shots on Target

Shots and shots on target are different statistics.

**Shots** represent the total number of recorded attempts.

**Shots on target** generally refer to attempts that are directed toward the goal and would enter the goal if not stopped by the goalkeeper or another relevant defensive intervention, according to the provider's definition.

A shot that goes wide or is blocked may count as a shot but not as a shot on target.

For example:

- Team A: 15 shots
- Team A: 5 shots on target

This means 15 total attempts were recorded, of which 5 met the relevant definition of shots on target.

The exact classification can vary between data providers.

## Example

Consider a match with these statistics:

- Team A: 15 shots
- Team B: 8 shots

Team A attempted more shots.

However, this does not automatically mean Team A created better scoring opportunities.

For example:

- Team A: 15 shots, 3 on target
- Team B: 8 shots, 5 on target

Team B had fewer total attempts but more shots on target.

This demonstrates why shot volume and shot accuracy provide different information.

## Shot Volume and Shot Quality

The number of shots alone does not tell the entire story.

A team may take many low-quality shots from outside the penalty area while another team creates fewer but higher-quality chances closer to goal.

This is why shot volume is often analyzed together with **Expected Goals (xG)**.

Shots answer:

**How many attempts were made?**

xG helps answer:

**How dangerous were those attempts?**

These are different questions.

## Shots and xG

[Expected Goals (xG)](xg.md) estimates the probability that a shot will result in a goal according to a statistical model.

For example, consider two teams:

- Team A: 20 shots, 0.8 xG
- Team B: 8 shots, 1.7 xG

Team A attempted more shots, but Team B created higher-quality opportunities according to the xG model.

This demonstrates an important distinction:

**Shots = volume**

**xG = shot quality**

A high shot count does not automatically mean a team created better scoring opportunities.

## Shots and Shots on Target

Shots on target provide additional information about the outcome or direction of attempts.

For example:

- 20 shots
- 5 shots on target

This tells us that the team produced a high volume of attempts but a smaller number met the provider's definition of shots on target.

However, shots on target still do not fully measure chance quality.

A shot from a difficult position can be on target while having a very low probability of becoming a goal.

This is why shots on target can be useful alongside xG.

## Shots and Possession

[Ball Possession](possession.md) can provide context for shot statistics.

A team with more possession may have more opportunities to attempt shots.

However, a team with less possession can still produce more dangerous attacks through transitions and counter-attacks.

For example:

- Team A: 65% possession, 8 shots
- Team B: 35% possession, 12 shots

Team B had less possession but attempted more shots.

This demonstrates why possession and shots measure different aspects of performance.

**Possession → Ball control**

**Shots → Attacking volume**

## Shots and Expected Assists (xA)

[Expected Assists (xA)](xa.md) measures the expected value of passes or actions that lead to shots, according to an xA model.

This creates an important connection between chance creation and shooting.

A simplified attacking sequence can be understood as:

**Chance Creation → Shot → Shot Quality → Goal**

xA can help describe the chance-creation stage.

Shots describe the attempt stage.

xG helps describe the quality of the resulting shot.

These metrics therefore complement rather than replace one another.

## Shots and Progressive Passes

[Progressive Passes](progressive-passes.md) help describe how a team advances the ball toward the opponent's goal.

A team that consistently progresses the ball into dangerous areas may create more opportunities to shoot.

However, progressive passes do not guarantee shots, and shots do not necessarily indicate effective progression.

Combining these metrics provides more context about how attacks develop.

## Shots and Passing Accuracy

[Passing Accuracy](passing-accuracy.md) describes how successfully passes are completed according to the relevant statistical definition.

High passing accuracy does not automatically result in more or better shots.

A team can complete many safe passes without creating dangerous opportunities.

This is another example of why football statistics should be interpreted as complementary pieces of information.

## Why Are Shots Useful?

Shot statistics can help analysts understand:

- Attacking volume
- Offensive activity
- Shooting frequency
- Team attacking patterns
- Player involvement in attacks
- The number of opportunities attempted

Shots can also provide useful context when compared with:

- Shots on target
- xG
- Possession
- xA
- Progressive passes
- Match results

However, shots should not be treated as a standalone measure of performance.

## What Do Shots Tell Us?

Shots can provide information about:

- How frequently a team attempts to score
- Attacking volume
- Shooting activity
- Offensive pressure
- Player involvement in attacking sequences
- The number of recorded attempts

Shot totals are particularly useful when compared across teams, matches, or periods of a season.

However, the number of shots should always be interpreted alongside shot quality and match context.

## What Do Shots Not Tell Us?

Shots do not directly tell us:

- How dangerous each shot was
- Where every shot was taken
- Whether the shot was likely to become a goal
- Whether the goalkeeper made a difficult save
- Whether the team created better chances than its opponent
- Whether a team deserved to win

For example, a team can record 20 shots while producing mostly low-probability attempts.

Another team may record only 8 shots but create several high-quality opportunities.

This is why shot volume should be combined with metrics such as xG and shots on target.

## Does Having More Shots Mean a Team Played Better?

Not necessarily.

More shots generally indicate greater attacking volume, but they do not automatically indicate better attacking performance.

Consider:

- Team A: 18 shots, 0.9 xG
- Team B: 9 shots, 1.8 xG

Team A attempted twice as many shots.

However, Team B created higher-quality opportunities according to the xG model.

The quality, location, and context of attempts matter.

## Why Can a Team Have More Shots but Lose?

A team may take many low-quality attempts while its opponent creates fewer but more dangerous opportunities.

Football results depend on goals, not simply shot volume.

A team can dominate the shot count and still lose if:

- Its attempts are low quality
- The opponent creates better chances
- The goalkeeper performs well
- The team fails to convert its opportunities
- The opponent is more efficient in front of goal

## How to Interpret Shot Statistics

A useful analysis can combine:

**Shots → Shots on Target → Shot Location → xG → Goals**

Each statistic provides a different layer of information.

**Shots**

How many attempts were made?

**Shots on Target**

How many attempts met the provider's definition of being on target?

**Shot Location**

Where were the attempts taken from?

**xG**

How valuable were the scoring opportunities according to the model?

**Goals**

What was the final outcome?

Looking at these statistics together provides more context than considering shot volume alone.

## Shot Volume vs Shot Quality

One of the most important distinctions in football analysis is:

**Shot Volume ≠ Shot Quality**

A team can produce many shots without creating many high-quality chances.

For example:

- 20 shots from difficult positions
- 0.8 xG

may represent less dangerous attacking output than:

- 8 shots from better positions
- 1.7 xG

This is why analysts often combine shot counts with xG.

## Data Methodology

Shot statistics are based on recorded attempts toward the goal.

Different football data providers may use different event definitions and classification rules when recording shots.

A shot classification may take into account factors such as:

- The player's attempt
- Whether the attempt is directed toward goal
- Whether the attempt is blocked
- Whether the attempt results in a goal
- The location of the attempt
- The match event sequence

The exact classification of an attempt can vary between providers.

### How to Compare Shot Data

When comparing shot statistics, consider:

1. The data provider
2. The definition of a shot
3. How blocked attempts are classified
4. Whether deflections are treated differently
5. The competition and match context
6. Whether the same statistical methodology is being used

### Important Note

Shot totals should be interpreted together with other metrics such as shots on target, xG, possession, and match context.

A high number of shots does not necessarily indicate that a team created high-quality scoring opportunities.

When comparing published statistics, refer to the original data provider and its methodology.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a shot in football?

A shot is an attempt by a player to score a goal.

### What does shots mean in football statistics?

Shots represent the number of recorded attempts made by a team or player to score.

### Are all shots shots on target?

No.

Shots can be wide, blocked, hit the post, or otherwise fail to meet the definition of a shot on target.

### What is the difference between shots and shots on target?

Shots represent the total number of recorded attempts.

Shots on target represent attempts that meet the relevant provider's definition of being on target.

### Does having more shots mean a team played better?

Not necessarily.

The quality, location, and context of the shots are also important.

### Why can a team have more shots but lose?

A team may take many low-quality attempts while its opponent creates fewer but better scoring opportunities.

### How is shots different from xG?

Shots measure the number of attempts, while xG estimates the probability or expected goal value associated with those scoring opportunities.

### Is a high number of shots always good?

No.

High shot volume can indicate attacking activity, but it does not necessarily indicate high-quality chances.

### Why are shots useful?

Shots provide information about attacking volume and shooting activity. They become more informative when combined with metrics such as shots on target, xG, possession, and chance-creation statistics.

### Can a team have fewer shots but higher xG?

Yes.

A team can take fewer shots but create higher-quality opportunities.

### Is shots data the same across every provider?

Not necessarily.

Different providers may use different definitions, event classifications, and data collection methodologies.

## Important Considerations

Shot statistics should be interpreted carefully.

Different providers may classify attempts differently.

Shot volume also does not automatically represent chance quality or attacking effectiveness.

For a more complete analysis, consider combining:

- Shots
- Shots on target
- Shot location
- xG
- xA
- Possession
- Progressive passes
- Match context

No single statistic explains the complete performance of a team.
